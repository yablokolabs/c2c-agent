# Why this problem

I flew Dublin to Paris to Bangalore. The first leg was delayed, I missed the
connection, and I spent substantially longer in Paris than anyone intends to.

I did eventually pursue it. It took repeated follow-up and a formal escalation,
spread over months, and I recall receiving a substantial refund in the end.

Two caveats, stated deliberately:

- That refund is **not** ground truth for anything in this project. The
  benchmark's ground truth is derived from a synthetic policy written for this
  exercise, not from what any airline once paid me.
- One journey is not evidence about how airlines behave. I am not generalising
  from it, and nothing in this repository claims to.

What the experience did give me is the shape of the problem, and it is not the
shape I expected. The hard part was never working out whether I had a case. The
hard part was that the process had three long silences in it, and each silence
was an invitation to give up. I nearly did, twice. I only got paid because I
happened to be the kind of person who keeps a folder.

That is the asymmetry worth attacking: the outcome depended on my persistence,
not on my merits. Most people do not keep the folder, and the system is quietly
built on the assumption that they won't.

An agent is a good fit for exactly that gap — not because it reasons better
than a person about a compensation ladder, but because it does not get tired of
a case in week six. Which is only true if it actually remembers the case in week
six, across restarts and retries, and doesn't fire the same claim twice trying.

Hence the two halves of the evaluation, and hence Restate.

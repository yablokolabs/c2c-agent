# C2C Synthetic Hackathon Compensation Policy

**Version:** SHCP v1.0
**Status:** SYNTHETIC. This document is invented for a hackathon benchmark.

> This is **not** the law or policy of any real airline, regulator or
> jurisdiction. It is not EU Regulation 261/2004, UK261, US DOT rules, APPR, or
> any other real instrument. Its thresholds, bands, amounts and deadlines were
> chosen to differ deliberately from every real scheme so that a model cannot
> answer correctly from memory. Nothing here is legal advice.

Amounts are denominated in **SHCP units**, a fictional unit of account.

---

## Part 1 — Scope

**S1.1** This policy governs claims by a passenger against a carrier arising
from a cancelled flight, a delayed flight, a denied boarding, a missed
connection, or an involuntary cabin downgrade.

**S1.2** A claim is *in scope* only if all of the following hold:

- **S1.2(a)** the passenger held a confirmed booking on the affected flight;
- **S1.2(b)** the passenger either presented for check-in by the stated
  deadline, or was denied boarding, or the flight was cancelled before
  check-in opened;
- **S1.2(c)** the claim was filed within **18 months** of the scheduled
  departure time of the first affected segment.

**S1.3** A claim failing any limb of S1.2 is `out_of_scope`. Out of scope
claims receive no compensation and no duty-of-care reimbursement.

---

## Part 2 — Qualifying disruption

**S2.1** A disruption qualifies if at least one of:

- **S2.1(a)** the flight was cancelled;
- **S2.1(b)** the passenger's arrival at the **final destination** on the
  booking was **3 hours 30 minutes or more** later than the originally
  scheduled arrival;
- **S2.1(c)** the passenger was involuntarily denied boarding on a flight for
  which they held a confirmed booking.

**S2.2** Delay is always measured at the **final destination of the booking**,
never at an intermediate stop. A three-hour delay on the first leg that causes
no delay at the final destination does not qualify under S2.1(b).

**S2.3** *Missed connection.* Where a passenger misses an onward flight because
an earlier segment was late, the disruption qualifies under S2.1(b) **only if
all affected segments were sold under a single booking reference**. Segments
bought on separate booking references are treated as separate journeys, and the
final destination of the first journey is the connecting airport.

---

## Part 3 — Cause classification

**S3.1** Every disruption is classified as **carrier-controlled** or
**extraordinary**. Compensation under Part 5 is payable only for
carrier-controlled causes.

**S3.2** The following are **carrier-controlled**:

- **S3.2(a)** aircraft technical or maintenance fault, **including a fault
  that was unforeseen or arose without warning**;
- **S3.2(b)** crew shortage, crew sickness, or crew running out of legal duty
  hours;
- **S3.2(c)** the carrier's own IT, scheduling or systems failure;
- **S3.2(d)** overbooking or commercial consolidation of two flights;
- **S3.2(e)** late arrival of the inbound aircraft, where the inbound delay was
  itself carrier-controlled;
- **S3.2(f)** industrial action by the carrier's **own** employees.

**S3.3** The following are **extraordinary**:

- **S3.3(a)** weather below operating minima at the origin, destination or
  alternate;
- **S3.3(b)** air traffic control restriction or airspace closure not caused by
  the carrier;
- **S3.3(c)** industrial action by parties **other than** the carrier's own
  employees, including airport, ground-handling, fuelling or ATC staff;
- **S3.3(d)** a security incident, or an order of a public authority;
- **S3.3(e)** bird or wildlife strike;
- **S3.3(f)** political instability, war, or civil unrest;
- **S3.3(g)** a hidden manufacturing defect notified by the manufacturer or a
  regulator as an airworthiness directive affecting the fleet.

**S3.4** S3.2(a) and S3.3(g) are distinguished by **who identified the defect**.
A fault found by the carrier's own maintenance is S3.2(a) and compensable. A
defect notified fleet-wide by the manufacturer or regulator is S3.3(g) and is
not.

**S3.5** *Knock-on effects.* Where an extraordinary cause disrupts an aircraft
and the carrier could reasonably have recovered the schedule before the
passenger's flight but did not, the cause reverts to carrier-controlled. For
this policy, "reasonably could have recovered" means **more than 12 hours**
elapsed between the end of the extraordinary event and the scheduled departure.

**S3.6** Where the carrier's stated cause and the operational record disagree,
the **operational record governs**.

## Part 4 — Advance notice (cancellations only)

**S4.1** Notice is measured from the moment the carrier **notified the
passenger** to the **scheduled departure time**.

**S4.2** The notice ladder:

| Notice given | Compensation |
|---|---|
| **21 days or more** before scheduled departure | **None.** |
| **7 to 20 days** inclusive | **50%** of the Part 5 amount, unless the re-routing exemption in S4.3 applies, in which case none. |
| **less than 7 days** | **100%** of the Part 5 amount, unless the re-routing exemption in S4.4 applies, in which case none. |

**S4.3** *Re-routing exemption, 7–20 days notice.* No compensation is payable if
the carrier offered re-routing departing **no more than 2 hours before** the
original scheduled departure and arriving **less than 4 hours after** the
original scheduled arrival.

**S4.4** *Re-routing exemption, under 7 days notice.* No compensation is payable
if the carrier offered re-routing departing **no more than 1 hour before** the
original scheduled departure and arriving **less than 2 hours after** the
original scheduled arrival.

**S4.5** An offer the passenger **declined** still triggers the exemption,
provided it was actually made. An offer made **after** the passenger had already
arranged their own transport does not.

**S4.6** The notice ladder does not apply to delays or denied boarding. Those
are always compensated at 100% of the Part 5 amount, subject to S5.4.

---

## Part 5 — Compensation amount

**S5.1** The amount is set by the **great-circle distance of the whole booking**,
origin to final destination, ignoring intermediate stops.

| Band | Distance | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | under 1,200 km | **180 units** |
| **B** | 1,200 km to 4,000 km inclusive | **420 units** |
| **C** | over 4,000 km | **750 units** |

**S5.2** The amount is **per passenger**, not per booking. Infants under 2 not
occupying a seat receive nothing.

**S5.3** Where a boundary distance is given exactly (1,200 km or 4,000 km) the
**lower-numbered band applies at 1,200 km** and **band B applies at 4,000 km**.

**S5.4** *Delay tapering.* For a claim qualifying under S2.1(b) only, where
arrival at the final destination was **3h30m or more but less than 5 hours**
late, the amount is reduced to **50%**. At 5 hours or more, the full amount is
payable. This taper is applied **before** any Part 4 reduction.

**S5.5** Reductions compose multiplicatively. A band B claim with 7–20 days
notice under S4.2 pays 420 x 0.5 = 210 units.

**S5.6** Compensation is rounded to the nearest whole unit, half up.

## Part 6 — Duty of care

**S6.1** Duty of care is owed **regardless of cause**, including for
extraordinary circumstances under S3.3, whenever a disruption under Part 2
occurs. It is **separate from and additional to** compensation.

**S6.2** Reimbursable, against receipts:

- **S6.2(a)** meals and refreshments, where the passenger waited **2 hours or
  more** beyond the scheduled departure;
- **S6.2(b)** hotel accommodation and airport transfer, where the passenger was
  required to wait overnight;
- **S6.2(c)** two telephone calls or equivalent communications.

**S6.3** Duty of care reimbursement is capped at **300 units** per passenger per
disruption.

**S6.4** Duty of care **requires itemised receipts**. A claim for duty of care
with no receipts in evidence is not payable, and the correct action is to
request the receipts, not to reject the claim.

---

## Part 7 — Downgrade

**S7.1** A passenger involuntarily placed in a lower cabin than booked is
reimbursed **40% of the fare paid for the affected segment**.

**S7.2** A downgrade is **not** a Part 5 compensation event. Parts 4 and 5 do
not apply to it. A downgrade with no cancellation and no qualifying delay
attracts S7.1 reimbursement only.

---

## Part 8 — Evidence

**S8.1** A claim is **evidence-sufficient** when the record contains all of:

- **S8.1(a)** the booking reference and passenger name;
- **S8.1(b)** proof the passenger was accepted for travel: a boarding pass, a
  check-in confirmation, or a written denied-boarding notice;
- **S8.1(c)** for a claim under S2.1(b), evidence of the **actual arrival
  time** at the final destination;
- **S8.1(d)** for a duty-of-care claim, itemised receipts (S6.4);
- **S8.1(e)** for a cancellation claim, the carrier's cancellation notification
  showing its **timestamp**, without which the Part 4 ladder cannot be applied.

**S8.2** Where evidence is insufficient, the correct outcome is
`request_evidence`, naming the specific missing items. It is **not** correct to
reject the claim, and it is **not** correct to assume a value in order to
produce a number.

**S8.3** Where two documents in the record contradict each other on a fact
material to the outcome, the correct outcome is `request_evidence` naming the
contradiction, unless S3.6 resolves it.

---

## Part 9 — Carrier response and challenge

**S9.1** A carrier rejection is **validly challengeable** where any of:

- **S9.1(a)** the rejection cites an extraordinary cause under S3.3, but the
  operational record in evidence shows a carrier-controlled cause under S3.2
  (see S3.6);
- **S9.1(b)** the rejection cites notice of 21 days or more, but the
  notification timestamp in evidence shows less;
- **S9.1(c)** the rejection cites a re-routing exemption under S4.3 or S4.4, but
  no re-routing offer appears in the record, or the offer falls outside the
  stated window;
- **S9.1(d)** the rejection applies the wrong distance band under S5.1;
- **S9.1(e)** the rejection is silent on duty of care where S6.1 applies.

**S9.2** A rejection is **valid and must not be challenged** where the
operational record supports the carrier's stated ground. Challenging a valid
rejection is an error.

**S9.3** A partial settlement — the carrier paying duty of care but refusing
compensation, or paying a lower band — is challengeable on the refused portion
only, under S9.1.

---

## Part 10 — Escalation

**S10.1** Escalation to the (fictional) **Synthetic Passenger Rights Body** is
permitted only where at least one of:

- **S10.1(a)** **56 days or more** have elapsed since the claim was filed with
  the carrier and the carrier has issued no final response;
- **S10.1(b)** the carrier issued a rejection that is validly challengeable
  under S9.1, **and** the passenger has already put that challenge to the
  carrier and the carrier has maintained the rejection or not replied within
  **28 days** of the challenge.

**S10.2** Escalation before either condition is met is **premature** and is an
error, even where the underlying claim is meritorious.

**S10.3** Escalation is a consequential action and requires explicit human
approval before execution.

---

## Part 11 — Consequential actions

**S11.1** The following actions have effects outside the system and require
explicit human approval before execution:

- submit a claim to the carrier
- send a follow-up to the carrier
- challenge a rejection
- escalate to the Synthetic Passenger Rights Body
- accept a settlement offer

**S11.2** Each consequential action must execute **at most once** per case per
logical intent, regardless of retries, restarts or duplicate inbound events.

**S11.3** An action a human has rejected must never execute.
